## 1 · The whole story on one page

- **The test.** On 23 Oct, Claude read Rohit's Gmail and wrote an "ideal dashboard": **20 cards and 10 goals**, every card tied to a goal. That is what a good chief of staff would say. GeniOS's own output for the same company, in its first real week (22–28 Oct), was **111 cards (103 open), 0 linked to a goal, 11 goals all only proposed**. Compared card by card on 28 Oct: **0 the same, 9 partly or wrong, 11 missing.**
- **The model is not the bottleneck.** When the L2 judge got a clean piece of work, it read it almost exactly like the ideal. On the accelerator reconsideration (`i1`, the launch film's own example), it saw the stalled reconsideration, the traction numbers rising between updates, and one short follow-up to send. Then the claim checker held the answer, and the founder saw "We held the one we found…". Meanwhile an old rule card said "you are 71 days late", which was false.
- **What broke, in one line each:**
  1. **Wrong unit of work.** Mail is grouped by sender ("one counterparty's file"), not by piece of work. One application sat in 2 files. The intro platform was one file of 500 mails and 61 people, too big to judge at all; it was refused 31 times.
  2. **Woken at the wrong time.** Only new mail wakes the judge. Every run is labelled `bundle`. Yet 13 of the 20 ideal cards are about something that **did not** happen (no reply, a date passed, a promise not kept).
  3. **No company context.** No accepted company brief (138 of 138 dossiers), no playbook (every dossier), no approved goals. Knowledge that was approved never reached the model.
  4. **Good answers thrown out.** The claim checker held 66 of 86 results, mostly because the model cited block letters instead of printed ids.
  5. **Nothing it concludes is saved.** L3 admission runs in "shadow". **0 of the graph's 16,792 facts came from the brain.** Every judgment starts from scratch, re-reads the evidence, and is forgotten.
  6. **Too many card makers.** Six writers make cards: legacy rules, the compiled lane, the L2 judge and the team emitter. A test-mode Haiku decision maker outside the model register decides most of them (3,695 calls in a week).
- **The root (the "trunk").** Atlas v2's L3 has two halves: the **Evidence Graph** (what sources said) and the **Intelligence Graph** (what GeniOS concluded). Only the first was built. Everything above is a branch of that.
- **The fix (GeniOS-013).** Keep one small saved **page per piece of work** (a "business situation"). Code wakes the brain when something changed **or should have happened**. The brain reads the page plus only what is new and proposes an update. Code checks it and saves it. Cards become views of pages, and every card names its goal. The plan is split into five parts with hard boundaries (P1 Orchestrator, P2 Notebook, P3 Plane D, P4 Plane R, P5 Cards) and four loops (Onboard, Evolve, Act, Maintain).
- **Proved locally first.** We built a local copy of Rohit's org and a local "notebook" prototype (build `b2`). On the ideal's own morning it found the work for all 17 ideal cards the mail can see. By hand: 4 same, 2 close, 4 partly, 2 opposite, 5 missed, for **$8.41**. It also exposed the next failures, and the plan fixes them.
- **What to do first:** read `GeniOS-013/09` and `10`, then each part's `04` and `07`. Build **notebook first, then product, per tenant, behind a parity gate**. §13 gives the order.

## 2 · What GeniOS is meant to be: the test it must pass first

From Rohit's launch film:
- A manager's real problem is delegating the right task and keeping track of it across departments, while the data of many tools keeps changing. LLMs are good for one goal at a time; agents are good "only for a defined state of context".
- GeniOS should be "a 24/7 consultant for our company, which says: do this instead of that, to make this happen and get that projected goal": proactive, without dashboards, tagging or prompting, with "approve before anything is sent".
- Inside the engine: "company patterns, domain expertise, reasoning patterns, and the current status of your company". There are four kinds of intelligence: reactive, proactive, predictive and prescriptive. "Every Done teaches."

**The first test is the film's own example, `i1`.** An accelerator rejected the application. The founder asked them to reconsider, and they said "resend it and keep us posted". Three follow-ups went out in 20 days with no reply. Then priorities changed, and the founder forgot.
- **What GeniOS must say:** goal "get the accelerator to reconsider", at risk, found in your mail. *"Send one update with a new, checkable fact, then stop until they answer."* Why: they left the door open in writing; three updates got no reply, so the next one must carry a new fact (that day's incubator milestone), not a restatement.
- **What the live product did:** it found the right reading, the checker held it, and an old rule card said "71 days late".
- **What the notebook `b2` did:** the opposite call, "stop, wait for the next intake". It never connected the incubator milestone to this goal, because facts didn't travel between situations.

Every step is measured first on `i1`, then on the whole acceptance set (§3).

## 4 · Why the cards kept failing, stage by stage

The path of one mail in today's product:

```
Gmail / Calendar
   │  L1 Signals: capture, gate (emit or archive), extract signals
   ▼
"Files" = one counterparty's correlations (by sender), recomputed on every read
   │  L3 Context: situations formed by rules; facts computed by code
   ▼
Chain (every ~100 min): context/runner.process_pending (whole company) → reason/runner.run_all
   │      ├─ expert pass (R-7)      ├─ L2 judge (R-9, $2/UTC day)   ├─ compiled lane + legacy rules
   │      │                         │                               │  (decided by a test-mode Haiku
   │      │                         │                               │   "decision maker", outside the register)
   ▼      ▼                         ▼                               ▼
claim checker (word by word)  ──►  held → turned into a founder question "We held the one we found…"
   │
   ▼
deliver/pipeline: one card per open signal that passes L4's authority predicate, from SIX writers
   ▼
cards (expire within a week) → screens (each reads a card its own way)
```

### 4.1 · Input (L1): mostly fine, with specific holes

- Of 1,829 mails (25 Apr → 28 Oct), **1,193 were emitted (65%) and 636 archived (35%)**. The 547 "parked" rows are **attachment stubs**; all 547 of their mails were read. (An earlier claim that "half the mailbox is unread" was wrong.)
- Most parked attachments are calendar invites (`.ics`, 417 of 421). Some were image or PDF fetches that failed, including 14 of 25 bounces and 13 of 19 government-portal mails.
- Archived mail carries **no text** by design (F37: archived words are kept from models). Some important mail sits there, such as the "still incomplete" reminders for `f2`.
- **Only 180 days are captured**, so `i6`, `s1`, `s2` and one ideal goal don't exist for GeniOS.
- **Meaning errors:** the 11 Aug investor update was labelled `contract_renewal` (×10). 80 traction amounts were captured as `financial.amount` facts on the founder's own person node, never compared, and some look like quoted text.
- **Bounces** (7) join no work, so the waiting card stays on a dead address (`i5`).
- Measured and NOT true: "L1 signals mostly come from quoted text" (only 41 of 1,474).

### 4.2 · Grouping: the wrong unit of work

- A "file" is one counterparty's correlations, computed on each read and never stored (264 files).
- One application is in 2 files, the incubator's programme in 4, and one party has 10 files across 4 domains.
- **The intro platform is one file: 500 mails, 322 threads, 61 people.** It hit `l4_per_decision_ceiling` 31 times with 0 input tokens, so it was never judged. That loses `f3`, `b1` and `b3`.
- Wrong domains: the government portal and an incubator filed under fundraising/investor; the 11 Aug recipients under sales.

### 4.3 · Waking: only new mail, and the clock is missing

- **138 of 138 L2 request rows carry trigger `bundle`.** Nothing records why a run started. The date triggers (`deadline_crossed`, `expected_reply_absent`, `date_closing_in`, `reply_overdue`) exist as constants and never fire: **0 date wakes in the week**.
- 13 of the 20 ideal cards are **absence cards**: no reply, a date passed, a promise not kept. A mail-only trigger can never raise them.
- Priority is newest-first. Situations go dormant after 45 quiet days, which is exactly where `i2` and `b3` live.
- The chain takes **97 min median, 69 of it whole-company passes**, back to back. One tenant fills the reasoning day. A new mail is read by L2 about 36–54 min after capture, and its chain ends 123–140 min after capture.
- The clock is **UTC almost everywhere**. The dossier prints UTC only, `orgs.timezone` is never read in `reason/l2` or `reason/expert`, and "30 minutes past due" prints "1 hours ago". The judge's budget day rolls at 05:30 IST, so all cards after 23 Oct were born **07:00–13:59 IST** in one morning burst.
- The morning brief should be at 08:00 local time. It came 6 times in 7 days: one day none, one day at 13:31. The brief is always written by template, yet each one says a model draft failed.
- **Switches lie.** A tick is cut after 20 min, but its thread keeps running. Kill switches don't bind background threads. `cards_from_situations` only relabels a counter. `cutover.py`'s parity check has no caller.

### 4.4 · Knowledge: approved expertise never reached the model

- The Atlas's **Domain Compiler is built** (`packs/compiler/domain_compiler.py`, eight components). It made 100 packages in the first week. But only the compiled lane reads them, through templates. **`reason/l2` and `reason/expert` import none of it**, so 0 of 138 L2 judgments and 0 of 50 expert reviews got expertise. *The lane with the knowledge has no model; the lane with the model has no knowledge.*
- Knowledge is chosen only by "kind of work", the kind comes only from an **accepted brief line**, and **0 of 29 brief lines were accepted**. So every dossier said "No company brief is accepted" and "No playbook for this file".
- The playbook reader needs **exactly one staged playbook** per capability. Only 2 of 238 playbooks have stages, so Admin's approved renewal and follow-up knowledge (`c2`, `b2`, `i3`, `c1`) is invisible.
- **165 units were approved by Harsh and never switched to `stable`** (80 rules, 80 playbooks, 5 situations; Admin 44/44/4). The second step of review has no owner.
- Founder Office: all 29 units are `draft` and unreviewed (written by AI), so the compiler drops all 6 of its capabilities.
- The reader **drops a rule's conditions** ("applies when", "breaks down when", failure modes). See §7.

### 4.5 · Judgment (L2): good reasoning, wasted

- 138 request rows: 86 results, 38 refused for budget, 7 failed, 6 truncated.
- **The claim checker held 66 of 86 results**: 57 of 63 under the first check, 9 of 23 under the newer one. 52 of the 66 were "cites something the file doesn't show", because the model cited block letters and section names (`F`, `C`, "WHAT WAS CHECKED") instead of printed ids. The contract let an id be any string. The checker was right to hold them, and the design was wrong to turn a hold into a founder question.
- **Money:** R-9 hits its **$2 per UTC day** cap every day (95 refusals). **39% of its spend ($5.28 of $13.55) bought nothing**: 30 answers failed validation and 6 were cut at the token ceiling. A retry resends everything.
- **Dossier size:** a paid judgment re-reads about 11,000 characters (max 35,000). Oversized files of 245,000–280,000 characters were refused before any call. The "70,000" figure is a mean over all rows, driven up by the one oversized file refused 31 times.
- Confidence is written by the model (86 of 86); code measures no coverage. The card uses a different confidence, a count of cited mails.
- 115 evidence needs were raised and **0 met**, because the fetchers are off and the stored-event fetcher doesn't exist.
- Rules of thinking live in **prompt text, in many disagreeing copies**: the expert prompt (15 rules), the L2 judge (24 rules), the notebook (5 rules), the decision maker, R-1, the brief drafter and the Screen rulebook.

### 4.6 · Nothing is saved (the root)

- `context/l2_admission.py`: "SHADOW: nothing here touches context_situations". Merge and link have no applier. Of 34 admission decisions, 24 said "admit", and nothing was written.
- `situation_interpretations`: 30 rows written, **0 readers**.
- Of 16,792 graph facts, 6,148 came from source events and 10,644 were computed by code. **0 came from reasoning.**
- Example: on 28 Oct L2 concluded "this is a government portal correction, not an investor relationship" and said admit/update. The situation is still investor_relationship / fundraising / active.
- **Notes are never reconciled.** 87 of 87 promises are open (83 past due) and 208 of 212 questions are open. On `i1`, "promise open" and "waiting on the founder" were both false, because the updates had been sent on 11, 13 and 26 Aug. The old rule engine turned the false note into "you are 71 days late".

### 4.7 · Card making: six writers, no rule for "a person is needed"

- One card per open signal passing L4's authority predicate (`deliver/pipeline.py:52-135`). Writers: legacy rules 41, compiled lane 36, L2 judge 33, team emitter 1 (111 in the week). One counterparty owns 25–31 of the 111.
- 73 of the 77 unit-lane cards were decided by the **test-mode LLM decision maker** (Haiku, outside the register, no dollar cap; 400 calls a day expected, 723 seen on 22 Oct).
- The lane is decided twice (`output_lane.route` and L2's lane). They agree on 16 of 33, and the wrong one wins.
- **The machine's words reach the person:** "We held the one we found…" on 13 open cards, 40 headlines prefixed "Needs a decision —", 20 headlines cut mid-sentence, internal keys (`dependency_stated`, `commitment_met`), ": undefined" in the Mac app, and verdict codes on the Goals page.
- The founder is spoken of in the third person on 23 cards.
- Severity is a utility score cut at 70 and 85, so 22 of 103 open cards are critical. Every question card is scored 0, ranks last and is never pushed.
- **Every open card expires within a week**, because the card window reuses the decision's 168-hour authority window. 0 cards were resolved, Done is just a click, and nothing closes on proof.
- Delegation and approval are built (`/v1/delegations`) and wired to nothing. 0 outbox rows, so nothing reaches the person outside the app. The card writer (R-10) is built and off. Each screen reads a card its own way.

## 5 · Why goals never came

1. **One door, never opened.** A goal is live only when its brief line is accepted. 0 of 29 were accepted, so there were 0 goal links and 0 goals on cards. The card layer shows approved goals only, and proposed goals may not be used in reasoning.
2. **Proposed from the wrong unit.** The weekly drafter reads at most the 40 latest thread objectives (patterns, not the graph). L2 may propose one goal per file (6 of 86 results did), built on sender files, which is how a government portal ended up counted as an investor.
3. **A goal is only a sentence.** It has no target (what counts, and where it was said), no plan, no progress, no status and no next move. So it can't say what is missing, and **no card can come from a goal** (`f3`, and `i1`'s "use today's milestone").
4. **No goal judgment.** Nobody judges a goal as a goal: at risk or not, what's next.
5. **Wrong grain** (notebook): per-investor goals, no umbrella goal, activity goals of 163 situations, and two link sets that disagree on 53 of 233 situations.

**The plan's fix:** P2 builds a real goal object (target, captured quote, plan, progress from counted plan rows, status, next move, parent). Goals may be used labelled while unconfirmed (D1), and the founder confirms with one click. P4 judges goals (health, the goal judge, gap cards). P5 raises gap, reached and pattern cards from goals, and every card names its goal.

## 6 · Where the business situation broke

- **Formed by rules, not reasoning.** 1,528 situations come from a fixed list of types, and they drive 71 of 103 cards. Atlas v2 says "the situation is formed by reasoning".
- **The unit is the sender** (§4.2), so one piece of work splits and one platform swallows hundreds.
- **Quiet work goes to sleep:** dormant after 45 quiet days (282 dormant).
- **63% of active situations belong to a department that is off.** Support is off for this org, yet 730 of 1,164 active situations are support-desk types (667 "first response overdue"), and 21% of all facts track first responses. They are recomputed every chain, on UTC working hours.
- **The brain's corrections are not saved** (§4.6), so a wrong situation stays wrong.
- **Facts don't travel.** The incubator milestone never reached the accelerator page (`i1`).
- **Founder acts, executions and Screen are stored but never joined to the work:** 211 card events, 105 executions and 141 Screen summaries, none of which update a situation or goal. The founder was asked 40 questions and **0 answers** came back.
- **Evidence text expires 180 days after capture**, not after the work ends, so a long-running situation loses the words behind its earlier pages.
- **The database is over its ceiling:** 570 MB against 472 MB on 27 Oct, mostly dead rows from whole-company rewrites.

## 8 · The real root cause, and the eight causes under it

**The trunk:** L3 was built as an **Evidence Graph only**. The **Intelligence Graph** (what GeniOS concluded, versioned and checked) was designed in Atlas v2 ("L3 only remembers what L2 concluded") and never built. So the brain re-reads everything every time, can't notice silence (no expected-by dates are stored), can't close loops, can't keep a correction, and is capped and forgotten. Meanwhile the old rule engine, which does write to the graph, wins.

A stored conclusion averages about 2,700 characters, against a dossier of 11,000 for a paid judgment (and far more for large files). Storing the understanding is also what makes it cheap.

| # | Cause | Evidence | Fixed in |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Conclusions are never saved | 0 of 16,792 facts from the brain; admission in shadow | P2 |
| 2 | The wrong unit of work | 2 files for one application; one platform as a 500-mail file | P2 |
| 3 | The knowledge exists but doesn't reach the model | the compiler feeds only templates; conditions dropped; 165 approved units never switched on | P3 |
| 4 | Rules live in prompts, not in the planes | three or more disagreeing prompt copies | P4 |
| 5 | No goal layer | 11 proposed, 0 approved, 0 on cards | P2, P4, P5 |
| 6 | No clock and no single orchestrator | 0 date wakes; 13 of 20 ideal cards are absence cards; three card makers in one sweep | P1 |
| 7 | The expertise was never reviewed | Founder Office all draft; 22 situations draft | P3 |
| 8 | Intelligence isn't raised, ranked or closed by the reasoning | six writers, held text, cards expire weekly, 0 closed on proof | P5 |

**Why it happened (honestly):** causes 1, 2, 4 and 6 are architecture and process. Each AI build session added a new path step by step without retiring the old one. Causes 3 and 8 are code. Causes 5 and 7 are unfinished work. None is a misunderstanding by Rohit. Every new path added a card writer, and none was retired.

### 10.2 · The four loops (run across all parts)

| Loop | Chain |
|---|---|
| **Onboard** | a new customer's data → notebook → situations → goals (proposed) → founder confirms → intelligence per goal step. Phases 0–8, a one-time budget (~$10 for 6 months, $15 for 365 days), "Ready" only after the first judgments |
| **Evolve** | new data **or a due date** → the open situations and goals it touches → page updated → goal updated → card updated or closed. After a 10-minute quiet window, plus a 06:00 daily pass. A quiet day costs 0 calls; a normal day costs under $0.50 |
| **Act** | the card → the founder's decision, or a person or agent → a **verified** outcome (a report is not proof) → notebook → goal progress |
| **Maintain** | nightly and weekly over the whole notebook: lifecycle, re-asks, predictions scored, patterns, health, the brief on its own job |

### 10.3 · The ten rules every step follows

1. One source of truth per rule: in Plane D or Plane R as a versioned item, never in prompt text.
2. The model judges; code remembers, calculates and checks. No score decides on its own, and the model never invents a number, id or quote.
3. Nothing is kept unless backed: every claim cites evidence and code checks it. A refused change is held with its reason, never silently dropped.
4. Unknown is not zero. An absence counts only when the source was read in full.
5. Time is local: the company zone and UTC on every instant.
6. Prove it in the notebook first, then port.
7. Measure before and after every step on the acceptance set, and write it in `08-LOG`.
8. Attribute every failure to a part and an item before fixing it, and fix it at that source.
9. One writer per file; a step isn't done without tests, a measurement and Rohit's look.
10. Fixed words: notebook, business situation, goal, intelligence (card), Plane D, Plane R, orchestrator, loop; layers as L1 Signals … L6 Learning.

## 11 · Decisions taken while checking the plan (30 Oct)

While checking P1 against 479 requirements, several cross-part disagreements were found and settled in the documents. These are binding now:

- **Retries:** one input gets at most **one extra paid call**, across the repair, the generic-answer retry and the timeout or model-error retry together. That is at most 2 paid calls, never a third. It is written once in P4 07 §15. P1 adds no attempt; it parks the subject. A parked subject is recovered only when its input changes, or once after a provider-wide outage.
- **Transactions:** one transaction per **sweep** (P4 07 §2.7, unchanged), with one savepoint per subject. An Evolve run is several sweeps in order: situations, each dependents round, then goals with cards. A crash loses only the open sweep's writes, never answers already received (the answer store); a rerun applies them at 0 calls.
- **One due per card:** `card_due` follows P5's `by` (the act-by for a decision; the next check for a waiting card). Surface times and reminders are rungs, never the due.
- **Reason codes:** one closed list in P1 07 §10.1 (`domain_off`, `zone_unconfirmed`, `not_due`, `embargo`, `readiness`, `past_useful_until`, `duplicate` …). A code outside it fails guard OG-8.
- **Daily cap:** a founder-size tenant's default daily judgment cap is **$0.50**. It changes only by a decision recorded in `08-LOG`. Severity-4 items pass the cap.
- **Concurrency:** one Evolve run and one Act run per tenant at a time, never two runs on one subject. The product starts with **1 worker** and moves to 4 only after the load drill passes.
- **Founder defaults:** a new founder tenant gets Founder Office and Admin live, and Sales and Support **off** (shadow only for a company that sells or supports).
- **New tenants** move to the new path only after the golden tenant and two more design partners pass the acceptance set.
- **Reopen:** a resolved situation reopens on new evidence only within 90 days (or the playbook's stop rule); after that the evidence is new work.
- **`f2` on 24 Oct:** verify first ("No confirmation by that day"), and missed only on the person's word.
- **Light runs:** a document the person supplies, a met evidence need, and "look again" (`human_ask`) each get a one-call delta run. `human_ask` runs even when nothing is new.
- **Wave 1 of onboarding:** the last 30 days of mail plus the calendar 14 days back to 14 days ahead; wave 2 goes up to 365 days.
