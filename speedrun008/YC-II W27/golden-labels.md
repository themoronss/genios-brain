## Why I answered as I did

- **`yes`** where somebody waits on you, a deadline or a meeting is coming, or an application you
  made moved — 1–7, 9–13, 15–17, 19, 24–29.
- **`brief only`** where the right behaviour is to know, not to act today: introductions nobody
  answered (8), an investor who asked for news only when there is real news (14), programs that
  wrote without asking anything (18, 20–23), and your own WhatsApp promises, which belong in the
  files they are about (30).
- **`no`** for 31–40: each is a shape GeniOS has already got wrong on your account (`04` §2,
  `STEP-01` §4).
- **41–44** (`STEP-04`): your company address is never someone you wait on (41); an ask that names
  you is about the person asking (42); your own pitch is named after the investor, never after you
  (43); an investor at gmail.com is an outside person, even though you are at gmail.com too (44).
- **45–47** (`STEP-10`): how fast you answer is a line in the brief, not a card (45); an answer in
  your other mailbox still reaches you, and your move is to reply (46); a bounced pitch reaches you,
  whatever shape the report comes in (47).
- **48–52** (`09` G1, the benchmark): someone waits on you in each — the person you were introduced
  to and never wrote to (48), the people you promised a booking (49), an investor whose question your
  template did not answer (50), the times nobody confirmed (51), and a fund that asked you to come back
  when a condition was met, now met (52).
- **53–55** (`09` G1, the benchmark): a relationship that went cold after real exchanges (53), events
  with no follow-up within a week (54), and weeks of no outbound while people waited (55) — each true of
  the founder across threads, so a line in the brief, not a card about one person.
- **56–58**: a renewal at a higher price is a decision with a date and money (56 — a plain receipt
  stays archived, row 33); an ask answered with the thing asked for is closed, and never raised again
  (57); your company's domain is also GeniOS's sending domain, and mail on it is still you (58).
- **59–64** (`STEP-14`, the Atlas's replay 02): three introductions are three files, a card each and
  none about the connector (59), even when they arrive forwarded in one thread — split per contact,
  never one ask (60); when one contact replies, that file's card moves on and the two silent ones stand,
  none closed by the other's reply (61); a booked call and a time only proposed stay in their own files
  (62); an introduction that leaves the contact's address out asks you one question — never a guessed
  address (63); a declined introduction clears its card and never comes back from old activity, while the
  connector's history is kept (64 — `no`: nothing to act on, and nothing may resurface).

A `brief only` answer is scored as *not expressible* until `STEP-15` builds the morning brief —
the board counts it apart rather than as a pass or a fail.
