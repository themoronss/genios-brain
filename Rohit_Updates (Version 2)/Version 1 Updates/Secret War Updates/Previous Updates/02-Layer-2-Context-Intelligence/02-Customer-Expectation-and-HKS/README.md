# Layer 2 — Context Intelligence: Customer Expectation and HKS

**Evidence baseline:** `harsh/mvp@b739bd5ca682d09550acc400ed2892c38c8518f8`, audited 2026-08-22. **HKS is preserved literally** as the supplied high-consequence scenario label; no expansion is invented. Layer 2 owns current-reality assembly—identity, role, relationship, timeline, conflict, permitted use and situation completeness. It must not invent expertise, choose the final action or claim a business outcome.

## Customer promise at this layer

The Customer should receive a bounded business situation, not everything ever observed about a person. Layer 2 must answer: who is requester, connector, target, owner and approver; in which relationship/thread/opportunity; what state is current; which evidence supports it; what conflicts or context is missing; and what visibility/use restriction survives. The Expected handoff is a versioned `BusinessSituationObject` that lets downstream expertise reason without reconstructing identity or chronology.

The current graph has meaningful Tested machinery: exact-key identity resolution with merge proposals, versioned fact authority, discrepancies, thread-first correlation, multi-domain projections, situation lifecycle and vector confidence. The focused Layer 2 suite passed **214 tests**. That proves specified cases, not Live tenant completeness or Outcome-proven customer value. The final BSO producer still reconstructs membership/evidence, hardcodes organization visibility, leaves relationships/dependencies empty, supplies neutral importance and reports no `missing_fields` (`context/situation_bso.py:69-166`). Therefore the customer promise is not yet guaranteed at the Layer 2 → Layer 3 boundary.

## Role and current-state contract

| Customer question | Required Layer 2 answer | Prohibited shortcut |
|---|---|---|
| Who is this about? | Exact business subject plus identity confidence and contested alternatives | Transport sender, connector or first same-name alias |
| Who did what for whom? | Typed requester/connector/owner/target/approver relationships | Flat person facts |
| Which work is still open? | Thread/opportunity-scoped unresolved request or commitment and superseding evidence | “Ball in court” stored globally on a person |
| What happened most recently? | Authoritative event-time sequence with source conflict and occurrence status | Latest ingestion time or old proposal |
| May this evidence be used here? | Narrowest ACL, purpose and excluded subjects on the BSO | Default organization visibility |
| Is context complete? | Required-field, source-coverage, identity and conflict vector | Empty checklist interpreted as complete |
| What can downstream trust? | Exact signal IDs, source receipts, graph/spec/extractor versions and missing-context codes | Synthetic evidence satisfying the contract |

## Customer acceptance boundary

Layer 2 is **Conditionally trustworthy only after** every HKS replay proves situation-scoped roles, current-state reduction, visibility propagation and explicit incompleteness. Acceptance requires zero connector-as-target errors, zero cross-role/cross-opportunity leakage, zero visibility widening, zero stale-completion claims, and 100% abstention when subject/role/current action is ambiguous. Live pilot metrics must include identity corrections, wrong-target rate, stale-loop rate, context-review burden, cross-channel resolution precision, source-coverage freshness and BSO missing-context frequency.

Until those receipts exist, the honest output is a bounded observation or `review_source`. A large graph, a high scalar confidence or 214 passing tests cannot substitute for the Expected customer experience: the right situation, about the right relationship, in the right current state, with permitted evidence and explicit uncertainty.
