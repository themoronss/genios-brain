# Layer 3 — Customer Expectation and HKS

**Evidence baseline:** `harsh/mvp@b739bd5ca682d09550acc400ed2892c38c8518f8`, audited 2026-08-22. HKS is kept as the project label used in this audit; this document does not invent an expansion for it. Customer expectations come from `00-Methodology/04-Customer-Intelligence-Contract.md`; Layer 3’s contract comes from `GeniOS-System-Design-Atlas.md:1262-1592`.

## What the customer actually expects from Domain Expertise

The customer expects GeniOS to behave like a seasoned operator who understands the **kind of situation**, not like a search result decorated with a YAML rule. Layer 3 must turn a role-correct `BusinessSituationObject` into an auditable `ExpertisePackage`: applicable capabilities, objects, professional rules, playbooks, mental models, failure patterns, evidence requirements, four-brain constraints and explicit coverage gaps. It must not choose the final action—that belongs to Layer 4—but it must make good choices possible and bad choices invalid.

“Deep Sales expertise” therefore means more than detecting silence or a meeting mention. It must distinguish prospect, investor, introducer, partner, customer and deferred/rejected relationship; determine what commercial or relationship loop is still open; apply company permissions and learned preferences without weakening policy; and expose what evidence is missing. If the situation is fundraising, support, admin or otherwise unsupported, forcing it through generic Sales is a product failure, not graceful reuse.

| Customer question | Expected Layer 3 answer | Current failure mode | Required behavior |
|---|---|---|---|
| What kind of relationship is this? | Role, domain, lifecycle state and thread-scoped subject | Email sender/domain is treated as the business subject | Require role provenance; keep connector, requester, target and owner separate |
| What remains unresolved? | Typed request, promise, decision, objection or cadence obligation | Any old inbound message becomes “Reply now” | Return an open-loop object only when completion evidence is absent |
| Which expertise applies? | Domain + situation + capability versions + coverage and review state | Legacy `SALES_V1`/`GENERAL_V1` supplies a generic template; the new resolver admits any `stub:false` draft | Compile only reviewed/accepted capability versions or explicitly return unsupported/partial coverage |
| What company rules bind it? | Organization permission and policy entries | No customer-visible proof that the legacy card used the Company Brain | Pin selected entries and exclusions into the package receipt |
| Does the advice fit how we work? | Adaptive, Organization, Behavior and Expert preference resolution | Generic cadence and channel advice | Use Adaptive → Organization → Behavior → Expert for preferences only |
| Can a learned habit override policy? | Never; permission uses Organization → Expert | Learned behavior can be mistaken for authority outside the compiler | Reject Behavior/Adaptive permission claims and surface the conflict |
| How certain is the expertise? | Coverage, missing evidence, conflicts and source freshness | One scalar confidence conceals absent capability coverage | Separate evidence confidence from expertise coverage and downstream decision confidence |
| What if the domain is not covered? | `INSUFFICIENT_CONTEXT`, review source, or observation-only package | Unknown work is squeezed into Sales hygiene; an all-stub route throws only after resolution | Fail closed; no action-authorizing playbook |
| Was this expertise human-accepted? | Review state, reviewer, acceptance timestamp, version and content hash | A non-stub authored draft is mechanically treated as executable | Block prescriptive authority until review-state admission is satisfied |

## HKS scenario tests

These are killer scenarios because a superficially plausible reminder can be strategically wrong. Each scenario is an acceptance test for the expertise package, not merely a prompt example.

**Evidence classes: [CUSTOMER], [SCREENSHOT], and [MODELLED]** identify supplied requirements, visible symptoms and constructed pressure tests; `[CODE]` appears only where the pinned implementation itself supplies the condition. **Every HKS row names harm, required layers, prohibited output, fail-closed result, golden replay, and exit gate** so a scenario cannot pass merely by sounding plausible.

## What the package must expose to Layer 4

| Required field | Why the customer needs it | Fail-closed condition |
|---|---|---|
| Business subject and role graph | Prevents connector/target/requester confusion | Ambiguous subject or conflicting role provenance |
| Domain, situation and capability versions | Proves which expertise actually ran | No route, all-stub capability set, unsupported domain, or unaccepted draft |
| Review/admission receipt | Proves a named expert accepted the exact immutable version | Missing reviewer, unreviewed state, or content hash that differs from accepted hash |
| Expert objects and applicable rules | Gives reasoning typed concepts rather than raw text | Required object absent or unauthored |
| Candidate playbooks and failure patterns | Enables alternatives and prevents known bad moves | No relevant playbook must not become a generic imperative |
| Evidence requirements and receipts | Shows what is known, stale, conflicting or missing | Missing decisive evidence lowers authority, not merely a score |
| Four-brain snapshot and resolution trace | Makes company policy and preferences reproducible | Visibility violation or unresolved permission conflict |
| Coverage and exclusions | Stops “46 files” being presented as 46 working skills | Stub, draft/unreviewed, all-stub-route and unreachable material is labelled, never counted as active expertise |

## Ownership boundary

Layer 3 cannot repair a corrupted Layer 1 identity graph, invent a missing Layer 2 request, or decide the winning action. It must consume the BSO as evidence, reject malformed or unsupported inputs, and provide a sufficient expert frame. Layer 4 must rank options; Layer 5 must compile safe execution. This boundary matters because “more rules” cannot recover who Theresa is, whether Boardy was merely the introducer, or whether a meeting occurred.

At the audited commit, the architecture can represent this contract, but the active customer path does not prove it. The compiler is default-off (`platform/config.py:85-89`), reasons only in `ExecutionMode.SHADOW` (`reason/domain_shadow.py:103-125`), and its adapter sets `live_delivery_enabled=False` (`reason/adapters/expertise.py:190-213`). The count fields must remain separate:

| Domain | Total capability files | Stubs | Non-stub authored drafts | Reviewed | Accepted | Routed L2 types |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Sales | 46 | 43 | 3 | 0 | 0 | 19 |
| Customer Support | 49 | 40 | 9 | 0 | 0 | 5 |
| Admin | 57 | 57 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

Support’s registry contains 14 authored situations but routes only five L2 types. Sales has seven all-stub routed types and Support has three. Across the three domains there are **zero reviewed or accepted** capabilities.

The current compiler does not enforce the final part of that contract: `CapabilityResolver` skips `identity.stub: true` but admits every `stub:false` capability regardless of `status: draft` and `review_status: unreviewed` (`packs/compiler/capability_resolver.py:96-105`). A production admission gate must require a named reviewer and accepted immutable version; otherwise “deep expertise” is only authored reference material with executable reach.

Therefore the expectation gap is **activation + route coverage + authored depth + human acceptance + downstream semantic consumption**, not merely wiring. **Hash inclusion alone is not semantic influence; reviewed, accepted, and routed counts are separate fields**. A package hash proves identity/provenance changed; a typed consumer and a one-brain-at-a-time metamorphic replay must prove that a selected Company/Behavior/Adaptive value actually changed an allowed constraint, candidate, rank or wording before the product calls it applied judgment.

## Acceptance verdict

Layer 3 passes the Customer/HKS bar only when every golden scenario produces the correct domain and role graph, a commit- and version-pinned **accepted** expertise receipt, explicit coverage and review state, preserved permissions, relevant playbooks or honest abstention, and no fabricated relationship state. Until then, it is a credible expert-system foundation—not the deep, customer-visible expertise the cards imply.
